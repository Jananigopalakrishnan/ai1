Module 1: Autotrophs vs. Heterotrophs

Every living cell needs organic carbon and energy, but where and how they obtain them forms the grand ecological division of the living world.

A

Autotrophs ("Self-Feeders")

These organisms can construct complex organic chemical energy storage compounds (like glucose) directly from inorganic minerals, carbon dioxide, and ambient external energy (like sunlight).

  • Photoautotrophs: Harness photons (solar energy). (e.g. Trees, phytoplankton, cyanobacteria).
  • Chemoautotrophs: Harness energy from chemical bonds of inorganic compounds (e.g. Deep-sea sulfur vents).
H

Heterotrophs ("Other-Feeders")

These organisms cannot build their own carbon structures from inorganic compounds. They are dependent on taking in other carbon compounds by eating organic materials.

  • Consumers: Eat living autotrophs or other consumers (e.g. Deer, lions, humans).
  • Decomposers / Saprotrophs: Absorb organic energy from dead or decomposing organic waste (e.g. Mushrooms, soil bacteria).

Understanding the Absorption Spectrum

Chlorophylls (a and b) are the main pigments. They absorb blue and red light and reflect green light. This reflected light is why foliage appears green to our eyes.

Linear Pathway: Standard one-way pipeline. Electrons originate from water splitting (photolysis), pass down the ETC to Photosystem I, and are loaded onto NADP+ to form NADPH.

Linear vs. Cyclic Matrix

The Calvin cycle requires a precise ratio of 9 ATP to 6 NADPH molecules to synthesize one sugar G3P. The plant uses cyclic routing to balance these values.

Linear Photophosphorylation Triggers photolysis, releases oxygen gas (O2), and creates a balance of ATP and NADPH.
Cyclic Photophosphorylation Only cycles electrons around PS I. Does not split water, does not generate NADPH, but pumps protons to produce extra ATP.
Class Quiz Connection

"A targeted herbicide that blocks NADP+ reductase will halt linear flow completely, but leaves cyclic photophosphorylation intact to pump protons for ATP generation!"

Module 5: Evolutionary Adaptations

Compare how different ecological niches evolved divergent biochemical pathways to solve the Water vs. Carbon dilemma.

C3 Plants

Standard Strategy

No structural or temporal modifications. Performs both carbon fixation and the Calvin cycle in the same mesophyll cell at the exact same time.

Vulnerable Environment: Hot, dry climates where closing stomata triggers photorespiration.
Examples: Rice, Wheat, Soybean trees.

C4 Plants

Spatial Separation

Separates functions physically. Captures carbon in external mesophyll cells using PEP Carboxylase, then pumps carbon deep into bundle-sheath cells away from high oxygen levels.

Adaptive Environment: Bright, hot fields where water loss is a high risk.
Examples: Corn, Sugarcane, Crabgrass.

CAM Plants

Temporal Separation

Separates carbon processes by time. Opens stomata *only* in the cool night to trap CO2 safely, then processes carbohydrates via the Calvin cycle during the daytime.

Adaptive Environment: Arid deserts with extreme high daytime heat.
Examples: Pineapples, Cacti, Agave.
